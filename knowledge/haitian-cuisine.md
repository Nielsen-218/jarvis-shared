# Haitian cuisine — JARVIS cooking reference

Original reference (traditions and techniques, not copied from any book) so JARVIS can build
authentic Haitian-style dishes from what the household already has. Household note: 5 people
including an ~18-month-old, so always offer a toddler-safe, low/no-scotch-bonnet version.

## The flavor base: epis
`Epis` is the green seasoning base under almost everything. Blend to a paste and keep in the
fridge/freezer; use 2–4 tbsp per dish.
- Core: green bell pepper, onion, garlic, scallion, parsley, thyme, celery.
- Common adds: shallot, a little scotch bonnet (optional/omit for the toddler), lime juice or
  vinegar, olive oil, salt, black pepper, cloves, a bay leaf.
- Other staple seasonings: epis + lime/sour-orange marinade ("marinade/benyen the meat"),
  bouillon/Maggi-style cubes, whole cloves, parsley, thyme, scotch bonnet (piman bouk).

## Core techniques
- **Marinate proteins** in epis + lime/sour orange + salt (30 min to overnight). Rinse meat
  with lime/vinegar first ("netwaye vyann") — a cultural step.
- **Griyo/Griot:** pork marinated in epis + sour orange, boiled till tender, then fried crisp.
- **Sòs (sauce) braises:** brown marinated protein, then simmer in a tomato + epis sauce.
- **Diri (rice):** diri blan (white), diri djon djon (black mushroom rice), diri ak pwa
  (rice and beans) — beans simmered with epis, then rice cooked in the bean broth.
- **Sòs pwa:** pureed bean sauce (red/black/pinto) poured over white rice — great use-up for
  older beans or a can of beans.
- **Legim:** a thick stewed vegetable mash (eggplant, chayote/militon, cabbage, carrot,
  spinach) with meat — an excellent "use up wilting vegetables" dish.
- **Bannann peze:** twice-fried green plantains. **Bannann boui:** boiled plantain.
- **Pikliz:** spicy pickled cabbage/carrot slaw in vinegar + scotch bonnet — condiment, keeps
  for weeks, a great use-up for cabbage/carrots.

## Signature dishes (use as templates)
- **Diri ak sòs pwa** — white rice + bean sauce. Pantry-friendly, cheap, toddler-safe.
- **Poul nan sòs** — chicken braised in tomato-epis sauce.
- **Griyo ak bannann peze + pikliz** — fried pork, fried plantains, slaw.
- **Legim** — stewed vegetable mash with beef/crab, served over rice.
- **Soup joumou** — squash/pumpkin soup with beef, pasta, vegetables (independence-day dish).
- **Tchaka** — hominy/corn, beans and squash stew (hearty use-up).
- **Mayi moulen** — cornmeal (like polenta) with bean sauce or fish, often with avocado.
- **Pwason gwo sèl / fri** — whole fish, salted or fried, with epis.
- **Akra / marinad** — malanga fritters / spiced flour fritters (snack, use-up for root veg).
- **Bouyon** — a big mixed meat + root-vegetable + dumpling soup (ultimate use-up dish).

## Common Haitian pantry & produce
Rice, dried/canned beans (red, black, pinto), plantains (green & ripe), cornmeal, root
vegetables (malanga, yam/yanm, sweet potato/patat, cassava/manioc), chayote (militon),
eggplant (berejèn), cabbage, carrots, spinach/watercress (kresòn), tomato paste, scotch
bonnet, limes/sour orange, epis ingredients, bouillon cubes, pasta, cloves, thyme.
Proteins: chicken, pork, goat (kabrit), beef, fish, salt cod (lanmori), eggs.

## Use-it-up logic (reduce waste)
Map a soon-to-spoil item to the dish that absorbs it best:
- Wilting leafy/mixed veg, eggplant, chayote → **legim** or **bouyon**.
- Cabbage + carrots → **pikliz** (keeps weeks) or into **legim**.
- Ripe/overripe plantains → fried sweet plantains or **pen patat**-style; green → bannann peze.
- A can/bag of beans, older beans → **sòs pwa** over rice.
- Odd root vegetables (malanga, yam, cassava, sweet potato) → **bouyon** or boiled with sòs.
- Leftover cooked meat → into a **sòs** or **bouyon**; leftover rice → fried rice-style or tchaka.
- Squash/pumpkin → **soup joumou** or **tchaka**.
- Day-old bread/overripe fruit → **pen patat** / fruit as a sweet (not core, but zero-waste).

## Recipe output guidance (when generating)
Give: dish name (Creole + English), servings (default 5), total time, ingredients with
quantities (mark which came from the pantry/receipt and which to buy), numbered steps, and a
**toddler version** note (milder, softer, no scotch bonnet, cut small). Prefer dishes that use
the flagged "use-up" items first. Keep it budget-first; suggest substitutions when an
ingredient is missing.
